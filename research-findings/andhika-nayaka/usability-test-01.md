# Nayaka — early concept user testing

## What we showed

Nayaka presented the early web interface on a phone to show how it could work as a wearable. The explanation covered friend colours, lights that would change with direction or distance, prepared messages and customisation through a companion app.

The participants discussed the idea after the demonstration. Messaging and friend-finding were still simulated, and no physical ESP32 bands were tested.

## Quick messages

Participants liked having a quick way to tell friends they were going for food, using the toilet or taking a break. Loud music could make speaking difficult, and repeatedly taking out a phone could interrupt the performance.

They saw value in keeping these actions on a wearable with only a few simple controls.

## Friend lights

The lights needed more explanation. One participant was unsure whether the light showed where to turn, which direction a friend was in or how close they were.

This showed that the colour and light behaviour needed to make sense without someone explaining it during use.

## When people would use it

Participants thought the concept would be most useful when friends became separated. When everyone was already together, there was less need to check the device.

One participant would try it but was unsure about using it regularly because they did not attend concerts often. Building the real connection and tracking system was also seen as a challenge.

## What we learnt

- Keep messages quick and the wearable interaction simple.
- Make the meaning of friend lights easy to understand.
- Focus on moments of separation and reconnection.
- Move message customisation to the companion page.
- Test the real communication and tracking separately from the interface concept.

The discussion gave feedback on the idea and display. It did not establish working Bluetooth, accurate direction or successful use of the physical bands.
