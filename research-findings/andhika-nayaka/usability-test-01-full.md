## Concept presented

The moderator described a wearable intended to address three problems found in
earlier research:

- finding and reuniting with friends;
- communicating in loud environments; and
- reducing repeated phone checking.

The early interface used coloured indicators to represent different friends.
The team proposed changing those indicators as friends moved or came closer.
The concept also included pre-written group messages—such as going to the
toilet, getting food, or taking a break—with custom messages configured in a
companion app and synchronised to the wearable. The team intended to explore a
solution that did not depend on venue Wi-Fi or mobile reception.

## Findings

### What resonated

- A wearable feels faster and less disruptive than taking out and unlocking a phone.
- Pre-written messages directly address moments when speech is masked by loud music.
- Reduced dependence on venue reception was seen as relevant to a familiar problem.
- Participants preferred keeping the wearable interaction simple.

### Friction and uncertainty

- The light behaviour was not self-explanatory. A participant needed clarification about whether it represented direction, orientation, or proximity.
- Building a functional hardware or connectivity prototype was perceived as difficult.
- Likely adoption may depend on how frequently someone attends concerts.
- The device may deliver value only during occasional moments of separation, rather than throughout the entire event.
- A nearby team may be exploring related wayfinding territory, so the distinctive focus should be articulated clearly.

### Interpretation

The session supports the problem and basic value proposition, but it does not
yet validate usability. Participants reacted to a narrated concept and did not
complete representative tasks independently. Their questions nevertheless
revealed an important clarity issue: the friend lights need to communicate
direction and/or distance without verbal explanation.

## Recommendations for iteration 02

1. Label or animate the friend indicator so its meaning is understandable at a glance.
2. Decide whether the core cue represents direction, proximity, or both, then use a consistent visual model.
3. Test the three highest-value tasks: find a friend, send a quick signal, and understand an incoming signal.
4. Time each task and observe errors without explaining the interface first.
5. Keep the wearable interaction minimal; move infrequent setup and customisation to the companion app.
6. Demonstrate the low-connectivity story clearly, including what works offline and what technical connection is required.
7. Position the concept specifically around group reconnection and low-attention communication to distinguish it from general venue wayfinding.

## Suggested next-test prompts

- “You have become separated from your friend. Show me how you would find them.”
- “Tell your group that you are going to the toilet.”
- “A friend has sent you a signal. Explain what the wearable is telling you.”
- “Change the strength of the haptic alert, then turn it off.”

Record task completion, hesitation, interpretation errors, and confidence before
asking for general opinions.
