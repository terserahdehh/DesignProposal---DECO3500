## Survey results

Our survey received **27 responses**. Each person selected the one area that caused them the greatest difficulty at a concert or festival.

| Greatest difficulty | Responses | Percentage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Locating or reuniting with friends | 13 | 48.1% |
| Communicating with friends | 8 | 29.6% |
| Finding stages, facilities or other places in the venue | 3 | 11.1% |
| Using a phone while staying engaged in the event | 3 | 11.1% |

Finding friends and communicating were the two biggest problems, together accounting for 21 of the 27 responses. This is why we made Find Friend and quick messages the focus of PulseBand.

The written answers helped explain what was going wrong. People described weak or missing reception, calls they could not hear over the music, and difficulty typing in a crowd. Some moved away from the performance to find a signal. Others stayed together even when they wanted to do different things, or agreed on a meeting point because messaging was unreliable.

We chose a wristband so people could check a direction or send a short message without repeatedly taking out their phone. The arrow and proximity light were intended to give a simple idea of where a friend was and whether they were getting closer. Prepared messages let users send updates such as “Wait there” or “Meet me” without typing or trying to speak over the music.

The support question also backed these choices: 10 respondents selected a friend's approximate direction or distance, and 5 selected quick updates. These were design preferences, not tests of our prototype.

We wanted the bands to communicate without relying on venue mobile reception. Our physical bands send messages directly using ESP-NOW, while Bluetooth is used for setup. The arrow uses shared phone coordinates, so its accuracy depends on those coordinates.
