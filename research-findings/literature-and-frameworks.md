# Literature and design framework application

The two research PDFs supplied for the final documentation revision were read alongside the original social-wearables framework paper. Citations explain their relevance and limits rather than presenting their findings as PulseBand results. Full third-party papers are linked at their sources, not redistributed in this repository.

| Source | Relevant contribution | Application to PulseBand | Limit of the comparison |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lindström and Pettersson (2010), supplied as `1880071.1880113.pdf` | Festival observations identify ad-hoc re-planning and shareability as design challenges | Prepared updates should communicate intentions, support changing plans and let people control shared group information | A shared programme concept differs from our wearable. The paper does not validate our hardware, navigation or interface. |
| Larsen et al. (2013), supplied as `1306.3133v2.pdf` | Fixed Bluetooth scanners reveal mobility and grouping patterns at a music festival, with selective discoverability | Contextualises reconnection and the interpretive value of repeated proximity records. It also motivates attention to location retention and sampling bias. | Device co-occurrence is not a verified friendship, and the scanner deployment is not PulseBand's ESP-NOW pair or phone-derived direction. |
| Dagan et al. (2019) | Social-wearables framework asks who triggers input, intentionality, who perceives output and effects on social experience | Distinguish deliberate messages, opt-in ongoing location updates, wearer-visible signals, bystander visibility and the ability to decline | The framework concerns co-located social interaction. Our application to temporary separation within a shared venue is a design interpretation, not a replication of its evaluations. |

The [framework screenshot](../docs/assets/social-wearables-framework.png) was supplied by the team and is credited in [the process overview](../docs/wiki/Design-Process-Overview.md). [The prototype scope](../docs/PROTOTYPE-SCOPE.md) maps the questions to specific current behaviour. [Ethical Considerations](../docs/wiki/Ethical-Considerations.md) discusses control over activation, shared information and retention.

## Assignment context

The supplied `3_Design_Project_v02.pdf` is the course assignment brief. It is cited for the required research-to-evaluation design cycle. It is not evidence about festival attendees or the effectiveness of PulseBand, and its instructions are distinguished from the team's research findings.
