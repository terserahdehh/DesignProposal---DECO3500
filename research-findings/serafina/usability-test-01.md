# Serafina — early concept user testing

## What we showed

Serafina introduced the early PulseBand concept to two participants. The transcript labels them Participant 4 and Participant 5. These are the labels in this testing transcript, separate from the research interviews and later group-testing records.

The demonstration explained friend colours, brighter lights when approaching a friend, prepared messages, companion-page customisation and the wearable form. Some functions were still simulated. The participants gave feedback after the explanation; this was not a test of the physical bands or tracking accuracy.

## Participant 4

The participant liked the familiar message options, including Toilet, Taking a Break, Going Home and I'm Safe. Prepared messages made sense because typing on a small watch screen would be tedious. They also liked being able to customise messages for their own needs.

They responded positively to the idea of brighter lights when getting closer to a friend, particularly in a dark venue. They described the design as straightforward and liked its normal watch appearance and neutral white colour. They did not raise a specific concern during the discussion.

## Participant 5

The participant liked the simple menu and small number of buttons. They preferred the wristband idea to a necklace, which they expected to be heavier and more inconvenient. This was a preference based on the concept, not a comparison of physical devices.

Their main suggestion was to provide clear instructions before use. They suggested a visual demonstration or short video that could explain how the band works and also introduce it to potential users. They could see themselves buying it when attending with a group of friends.

## What we learnt

- Keep prepared messages and let users customise them before the event.
- Keep the menu and controls simple.
- A familiar watch-like design appealed to these participants.
- Provide a clear introduction to the controls and friend-finding cues, potentially through a short video.

The discussion supported the concept and identified a need for instructions. It did not establish that the location system worked or that users could complete tasks without an explanation.
